CPR/AED Certification Cost for Personal Trainers

Every certifier requires it and no certifier issues it. CPR/AED is a separate credential from a separate body, and its price swings more by provider type than most trainers expect.

CareerCost Guides · Updated September 2026

Our personal trainer certification calculator includes a CPR/AED line item. This guide breaks down why the price ranges so widely and which tier is actually worth paying for.

How much CPR/AED certification costs

CPR/AED certification typically runs $45 to $160, and the spread isn't random - it tracks almost exactly with who's teaching the class and how it's delivered. Most reputable providers, offering an in-person skills check plus a nationally recognized card, land in the $70-$120 range.

Typical cost by provider type

Ranges reflect typical listed pricing for single-session Heartsaver/BLS-equivalent CPR/AED courses. Individual class prices vary by city and provider.

What drives the price

Three things push a CPR/AED course toward the top or bottom of the range:

  • Who's running it. A fire department or community-college course is often subsidized and taught by volunteer or public-service instructors - that's the $45-$90 tier. A hospital-affiliated AHA training center, which carries more overhead, is the $100-$160 tier.
  • Format. Fully in-person classes and "blended learning" (online coursework plus an in-person skills check) are usually priced close together. Purely online-only options exist but many gyms and certifiers won't accept a card issued without a verified in-person skills check - check your certifier's requirement before booking the cheapest option.
  • Group size. Training centers commonly discount 10-30% for groups of 6 or more - worth asking about if you're certifying alongside other trainers from the same gym.

Watch for add-on fees on top of the course price: replacement or extra certification cards often run $10-$25, and some providers charge $10-$25 separately for printed materials. If a listed price looks unusually low, check whether the skills-check session and the certification card are actually included, or sold as separate add-ons - that's the most common way a $45 course ends up costing closer to $75 by the time you're actually certified.

American Heart Association vs. American Red Cross

Both issue CPR/AED cards that gyms and certifiers widely accept - there's no strong reason to prefer one over the other unless your specific certifier names one. AHA's Heartsaver CPR/AED course and the Red Cross's equivalent basic CPR/AED course are priced similarly at independent training centers, in the $70-$120 band. The bigger price swing comes from the provider type above, not the AHA-vs-Red-Cross choice itself.

Before you book a class, check these three things

  • Does your certifier accept it? NASM, ACE, and ISSA all require an AHA or Red Cross card specifically - a generic "CPR certificate" from an uncredentialed online course usually won't be accepted, even if it's cheaper.
  • Does it include a verified in-person skills check? Fully online-only cards are the cheapest option on paper, but most gyms and certifiers require proof of an in-person or live-video skills evaluation, not just a completed quiz.
  • Is your card still current when you apply for certification? Since CPR/AED renews every 2 years and most personal trainer certifications renew on a similar cycle, time your CPR class so it doesn't lapse partway through your certification application process.

Renewal: a recurring cost, not a one-time one

CPR/AED certification is valid for 2 years, matching the renewal cycle most personal trainer certifications already run on. Budget for it as a recurring line item alongside your NASM/ACE/ISSA renewal, not as a cost you only pay once at the start of your career. (Lifeguard-specific CPR certifications are the one common exception, typically requiring annual renewal - not relevant for most personal training roles.)

FAQ

How much does CPR/AED certification cost?

CPR/AED certification typically costs $45 to $160 depending on the provider and format. Fire-department and community courses run $45-$90, while AHA or Red Cross blended-learning courses from independent training centers run $70-$120 - the range most reputable providers land in. Hospital-based AHA courses can run $100-$160.

How often do personal trainers need to renew CPR/AED certification?

CPR/AED certification through the American Heart Association or American Red Cross is valid for 2 years before it needs renewing, which is the same cycle most personal trainer certifications (NASM, ACE, ISSA) follow.

Do personal trainers need CPR/AED certification separately from their trainer certification?

Yes. CPR/AED certification is issued by a separate body (typically the American Heart Association or American Red Cross), not by NASM, ACE, or ISSA. It's a required prerequisite most certifiers and gyms check for independently, and it has to be renewed on its own 2-year cycle regardless of your main certification's renewal date.
