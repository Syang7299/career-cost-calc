Texas Electrician License Cost & Requirements (TDLR Guide)

TDLR's own fees are small - the real story is an exam that fails roughly 3 out of 4 people who take it. Here's what the state actually charges at each license tier, and what the numbers say about how hard the journeyman exam really is.

Which license do you actually need

Texas licenses electricians in tiers, and each one permits different work:

  • Apprentice - no exam or prior experience required, but you can only work under direct supervision of a master or journeyman electrician while you accumulate hours.
  • Residential Wireman - a narrower, faster path limited to single-family and multifamily dwellings up to four stories. A good fit if you know you want to specialize in residential work and don't need the broader journeyman scope.
  • Journeyman Electrician - the standard, broadly-scoped license most electricians work toward. You can perform electrical work across residential and commercial settings, still under a master's supervision.
  • Master Electrician - the top individual tier, required if you eventually want to hold an Electrical Contractor business license and run your own crew rather than working under someone else's.

The journeyman exam is harder than most people expect

According to TDLR's own FY2024 exam statistics, only 27.86% of journeyman electrician exam attempts passed - 2,365 out of 8,490. That's roughly 3 out of every 4 attempts failing, and it's not a one-year fluke.

Journeyman exam pass rate, by year

Source: TDLR's own published exam statistics. FY2025's two bars reflect the exam's March 2025 restructuring into separately-scored NEC Knowledge and Calculations sections.

The exam got measurably harder in FY2025. TDLR restructured the journeyman exam in March 2025 into two separately-scored parts - NEC Knowledge (59 questions, 130 minutes) and Calculations (26 questions, 110 minutes) - and you need 70% on each part, not just an overall average. FY2025 data shows a 24.46% pass rate on the Knowledge section and just 20.56% on Calculations, both below the prior single-exam pass rates.

What this means for your budget

A failed attempt doesn't just cost time - the $78 exam fee is per attempt, so retakes add up. If you're budgeting for your path to journeyman in Texas, it's worth planning for the possibility of a retake rather than assuming you'll pass on the first try, given that roughly 3 in 4 attempts don't. A structured prep course before your first attempt is a small cost relative to a $78 retake plus the lost time waiting for the next available exam slot.

FAQ

How much does an electrician license cost in Texas?

TDLR's own direct fees are small: apprentice electrician is $20/year, residential wireman is $20/year plus a $64 exam fee, journeyman electrician is a $30 application fee plus a $78 exam fee, and master electrician is a $45 application fee plus a $78 exam fee. These are just the state's fees - your apprenticeship program (classroom instruction, union or trade school dues) is a separate cost, typically $250-$1,420/year.

How hard is the Texas journeyman electrician exam?

Harder than many candidates expect. TDLR's own FY2024 statistics show a 27.86% pass rate (2,365 passed out of 8,490 attempts). The exam was restructured into two separately-scored parts in March 2025 - NEC Knowledge and Calculations, each requiring 70% to pass - and FY2025 data shows pass rates of 24.46% and 20.56% respectively.

How many hours of experience do you need to become a licensed electrician in Texas?

Residential wireman requires 4,000 hours under a master electrician. Journeyman electrician requires 8,000 hours under a master. Master electrician requires 12,000 hours of experience plus a minimum of 2 years holding a journeyman license before you can apply.
