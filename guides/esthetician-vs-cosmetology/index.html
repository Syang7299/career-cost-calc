Esthetician vs. Cosmetology License: Which Should You Get?

Esthetician school can take a third of the time and cost of cosmetology school. But in most states, a cosmetology license already includes everything an esthetician license covers - the reverse isn't true.

CareerCost Guides · Updated September 2026

Our cosmetology calculator covers the full license. If you only want to do skincare work, esthetician is a genuinely different, narrower path worth comparing before you commit to the bigger program.

The scope difference is the real decision driver

An esthetician license covers skincare services only - facials, waxing, peels, and makeup application. A cosmetology license covers hair, skin, and nails, a broader scope that includes everything an esthetician does, plus haircutting, coloring, and nail services. That asymmetry matters: in most states, a licensed cosmetologist can already legally perform esthetician-scope work without a separate license, but a licensed esthetician cannot perform hair or nail services without additional training and a separate license.

Hours required: a real gap, not a small one

Per a state-by-state breakdown of esthetician licensing requirements, required hours range from 220 (Florida, the lowest in the country) to 1,000+ (Georgia), with most states landing between 600-750.

Required training hours by license type

Ranges vary by state - Florida requires as little as 220 esthetician hours, Georgia requires 1,000. Most states fall between 600-750 for esthetician programs.

Notice where the two ranges meet: the high end of esthetician requirements (1,000 hours in states like Georgia) lands right at the low end of cosmetology requirements. That's not a coincidence - it reflects how much additional training genuinely goes into the broader cosmetology scope on top of skincare alone. If your state happens to be on the low end for esthetician (Florida's 220 hours, for example) and the high end for cosmetology, the practical time difference between the two paths in your specific state can be even larger than the national ranges suggest - worth checking your own state's exact numbers rather than assuming the midpoint applies.

Where each license typically leads to work

Estheticians commonly work in day spas, dermatology and medical offices, and standalone skincare studios - settings built around facials, peels, and skin treatments specifically. Cosmetologists work more often in full-service salons, where clients expect hair services as the core offering with skin and nail services as add-ons. If you already know which setting you want to work in, that's often a faster way to decide than comparing hours and cost alone - a dermatology-office job typically wants an esthetician license specifically, while most salon-floor positions expect the full cosmetology license.

Income doesn't differ as much as the cost does

Entry-level estheticians typically earn $25,000-$32,000 in their first two years - close to the $20,000-$30,000 entry-level range our cosmetology calculator uses. Since the income gap between the two is small while the cost and time gap is large, the real decision isn't about which pays more starting out - it's about whether you actually need the broader scope. If your business plan is skincare-focused (facials, waxing, a spa-style practice), esthetician gets you there for meaningfully less money and time. If you want to offer hair or nail services too, cosmetology is the only path that covers it - esthetician alone won't get you there.

Before you decide: confirm your specific state's rule on whether cosmetologists can perform esthetician-scope work without a separate license - it's the case in most states, but not universally, so verify with your state board before assuming the broader license covers everything. It's also worth checking whether your state offers a shorter "path-up" track from esthetician to cosmetology that credits your existing hours, rather than assuming you'd have to start the full program from zero.

FAQ

What's the difference between an esthetician and a cosmetologist?

An esthetician is licensed for skincare services only - facials, waxing, peels, and makeup application. A cosmetologist's license covers hair, skin, and nails, a broader scope that includes everything an esthetician does plus haircutting, coloring, and nail services.

Which is cheaper: esthetician or cosmetology school?

Esthetician school is typically cheaper and faster: $3,000-$15,000 in tuition over 220-1,000 required hours (600-750 is most common), versus cosmetology's $5,000-$20,000 over 1,000-1,600 hours. The gap exists because cosmetology covers more services and requires proportionally more training time.

Can an esthetician become a cosmetologist later, or vice versa?

In most states, a cosmetology license already covers everything an esthetician license covers, so a licensed cosmetologist doesn't need a separate esthetician license to perform skincare services. Going the other direction - esthetician to cosmetologist - typically means completing the additional hair and nail training hours, since the esthetician license doesn't cover those services on its own.
