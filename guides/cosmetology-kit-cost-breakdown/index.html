Cosmetology Kit Cost: What's Actually Inside It

The national average cosmetology student kit costs about $1,693 - but that number hides a huge range. Depending on the state and school, kits run anywhere from $505 to $2,584. Here's what's actually in the box, why the price swings so much, and what happens to that number once you graduate.

CareerCost Guides · Updated September 2026

Our cosmetology calculator uses a flat $800-$2,000 line for kit and supplies. That's a fair starting-point range, but it's worth knowing what's actually being priced - because the gap between a $505 kit and a $2,584 kit isn't random, and whether that cost gets bundled into your school's advertised tuition or billed separately can change how two schools' price tags compare.

What's actually in the kit

Contents vary by school and vendor, but a typical cosmetology student kit includes:

  • Cutting shears - usually more than one pair (a standard shear plus a thinning/texturizing shear), in a protective case
  • Blow dryer, curling iron, and flat iron - the three core hot tools, sometimes name-brand (GHD-level packages exist at higher-tier schools)
  • Combs and brushes - cutting combs, rat-tail combs, wide-tooth combs, round and paddle brushes
  • Sectioning clips, perm rods, and coloring caps
  • Apron and multiple capes - cutting cape, coloring cape, often with a utility-pocket styling apron
  • Mannequin heads - usually more than one, often with real human hair for realistic cutting and coloring practice
  • Textbooks or a tablet - either physical state-board prep materials, or a tablet with access to the school's digital learning platform

Some schools add school-branded extras on top of that core list - T-shirts, jackets, business cards, a student ID - which is one of the reasons two "kits" at two different schools aren't actually the same product.

Why the price swings 5x by state

Two things drive most of that gap, and neither one is arbitrary:

  • Shear quality tier. Entry-level student shears run about $30-$150, mid-range shears run $150-$300, and professional-grade shears can run $200-$800 or more. A kit built around a single pair of entry-level shears and one built around a professional-grade pair with a backup thinning shear can differ by hundreds of dollars before anything else in the box changes.
  • Hot tool and brand tier. A basic blow dryer and iron might run $100-$150 combined; a name-brand hot-tool set (the kind some schools bundle in as a selling point) can run $300-$400 or more on its own. Add a school-issued tablet, printed textbooks, and branded merchandise, and a "kit" starts looking more like a bundled enrollment perk than a simple tool set.

Neither driver means the pricier kit is a better deal - a $2,500 kit with brand-name hot tools you'll replace within a few years isn't automatically worth more than a $600 kit with the same functional tool categories in a plainer brand. It's worth asking your school exactly what's included before assuming the price reflects quality.

Bundled into tuition, or a separate bill?

There's no consistent standard across schools. Some fold the kit cost directly into the advertised tuition number; others bill it as a separate charge due before your first day of class - on top of tuition, not included in it. This matters more than it sounds: a school quoting "$14,000 tuition" and a school quoting "$12,300 tuition" aren't actually the cheaper-looking offer if the second number doesn't include a $1,700 kit that gets billed separately later.

Before comparing two schools' price tags, ask the admissions or financial aid office directly: "Does this tuition figure include the student kit, or is that billed separately?" It's a simple question that a surprising number of published tuition comparisons skip entirely.

This is also exactly why our cosmetology calculator keeps kit cost as its own input line instead of folding it into tuition - so you can enter your specific school's real numbers for each, however that school happens to bill them, and still get an apples-to-apples total.

After graduation: do you need a whole new kit?

Usually not entirely. Most schools let you keep your student kit, and it becomes your starter set for real paying clients once you're licensed. What actually needs replacing over time:

  • Shears - when they go dull or the tension goes off, typically needing professional resharpening (roughly $10-$20 per pair) or eventual replacement, not on a fixed schedule
  • Combs and brushes - commonly every 6-12 months with regular salon use
  • Electric tools - blow dryers and irons typically last 1-3 years with proper care before performance noticeably drops

If you're building a full professional kit from scratch rather than starting from your student kit - say, you're upgrading everything at once a couple of years in - that typically runs $800 to $2,000, which happens to match the range our calculator already uses for its kit line. That's not a coincidence: the calculator's figure reflects a realistic working stylist's kit, not just a bare student starter set, so it holds up as a reasonable number whether you're pricing your first kit or your first real upgrade.

Budget note: if your school's kit fee is unusually high (well above the $1,693 national average), ask what's driving it - name-brand hot tools and bundled merchandise are legitimate reasons, but it's worth confirming rather than assuming.

FAQ

What's included in a cosmetology student kit?

Cutting shears, a blow dryer, curling and straightening irons, combs and brushes, sectioning clips, an apron and capes, perm rods, and mannequin heads, plus textbooks or tablet access to digital coursework. Exact contents and brands vary by school - some bundle designer hot tools and school-branded gear, others provide a bare-bones set.

How much does a cosmetology kit cost?

The national average is about $1,693, but it ranges widely by state - roughly $505 in Wyoming up to $2,584 in Kansas. Pre-assembled kits from individual vendors run $240-$790, while some private schools charge $3,000 or more for a fully branded package.

Is the student kit included in cosmetology school tuition?

It depends on the school - there's no industry-wide standard. Some fold the kit cost into the advertised tuition price, others bill it as a separate upfront charge before your first class. Always ask the admissions or financial aid office directly whether a tuition quote includes the kit, since two schools' "tuition" numbers aren't comparable if one includes it and one doesn't.
