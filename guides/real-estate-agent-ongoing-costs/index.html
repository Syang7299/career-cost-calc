The Real Cost of Being a Real Estate Agent (After You're Licensed)

Every real estate cost calculator, including ours, stops the day you get your license. But that's when the recurring bills actually start - dues, MLS access, a lockbox key, and insurance, every single year you stay active.

Sample total annual bills, by setup

"Minimal setup" skips MLS membership (limited to public listing access); "Typical REALTOR" is a mid-size market with individual E&O; "High-cost + cloud" combines an expensive MLS market with a cloud brokerage's flat monthly fee.

The biggest variable is your brokerage model

Dues and MLS fees are relatively fixed once you pick a market. Brokerage fees are where the real range comes from:

  • Traditional split brokerage: often $0 in separate fees - the brokerage takes a percentage of each commission instead.
  • Desk fee model: a flat monthly fee (commonly $50-$300) in exchange for keeping a higher share of your commission.
  • Cloud brokerage: a flat monthly technology/platform fee (around $85/month at one major cloud brokerage, roughly $1,020/year) with little to no office overhead.

None of these models is objectively cheaper - a desk fee or cloud fee can cost more per year than a split model if you close few deals, but less than a split model if you close many. The math only works out in your favor at a certain transaction volume, so it's worth running your own numbers before picking a model based on the fee alone. New agents in particular tend to underestimate how few deals they'll close in year one, which is exactly when a split model's low fixed cost matters most - a desk fee that looks cheap on a brochure can be the more expensive choice in your slowest year.

A worked example: split vs. desk fee

Say a traditional brokerage takes a 30% split of your commission, while a desk-fee brokerage charges a flat $200/month ($2,400/year) and lets you keep 100%. On a $6,000 average commission per closed deal, the 30% split costs you $1,800 per transaction - meaning you'd need to close fewer than 1.33 deals a year for the split model to cost less than the flat $2,400 desk fee. Close 2 or more deals a year, and the desk fee model keeps more of your money; close fewer than that, and the split model's lower fixed cost wins. This is exactly the kind of comparison worth running with your own real transaction volume and commission size before choosing a brokerage model based on the sticker price of the fee alone.

FAQ

What does it cost to maintain a real estate license each year?

Total annual cost typically runs $560-$3,500+, depending heavily on your market and brokerage model. A minimal, non-MLS-member setup can run as little as $558/year. A typical full REALTOR setup - NAR/state/local dues, MLS access, a lockbox key, and individual E&O insurance - runs roughly $1,000-$1,700/year. A high-cost market with a cloud brokerage fee can exceed $3,500/year.

What's the biggest ongoing cost variable for real estate agents?

Brokerage fees and MLS costs, by far. Brokerage models range from a traditional commission split with no separate fee, to a flat monthly desk fee, to a cloud brokerage's flat fee (around $85/month at one major cloud brokerage). MLS fees alone range from about $410 to over $700/year depending on which regional MLS system you're on.

Does the real estate calculator include these ongoing costs?

No. Our real estate calculator covers the one-time cost of getting licensed - course, exam, application, background check, and first-year E&O insurance. The costs in this guide are separate, ongoing costs that continue every year you stay active, on top of that one-time total.
