NASM vs. ACE vs. ISSA: Personal Trainer Certification Cost Compared

Every certifier's own comparison page happens to make itself look best. Here's what each actually charges on their own official pricing pages right now, the real exam-format differences, and how swapping any of these into our calculator changes your payback time.

The exam formats are genuinely different

  • NASM: proctored, closed-book, in-person at a testing center. Roughly an 85% first-attempt pass rate.
  • ACE: the default included exam is open-book and non-proctored - and notably, not itself NCCA-accredited. ACE's NCCA-accredited exam is a separate proctored option sold as an add-on.
  • ISSA: the standard exam is open-book, untimed, and taken online. Its NCCA-accredited pathway runs through a separate subsidiary (NCCPT), not the standard exam.

If a specific gym or insurer requires NCCA accreditation, check which exact exam version you're buying - "NCCA-accredited pathway available" and "this exam is NCCA-accredited" are not the same claim, and ACE and ISSA both sell a cheaper, non-accredited default alongside their accredited option.

Renewal costs don't stop at the initial price

All three require recertification roughly every 2 years, and none of them are free to renew. NASM's renewal typically runs $99-$129 plus continuing education units - the same figure already used as the renewal estimate in our certification calculator. ACE requires 20 continuing education hours per 2-year cycle, sold either individually or bundled into a renewal package. ISSA also requires periodic renewal with its own CE requirement. None of this is included in any certifier's advertised "get certified for $X" price, so budget it as a recurring cost on top of whichever initial package you choose - the same distinction our calculator already draws between the one-time certification total and ongoing renewal.

Which one actually fits how you learn

Since all three get you hired at a typical commercial gym, the more useful question is format fit, not brand. If you want a fixed, proctored exam date that forces you to actually finish studying, NASM's structure does that by design. If you want the flexibility to study and test entirely on your own schedule without driving to a testing center, ISSA's standard online open-book exam is built for that - though check whether your target employer specifically wants the NCCA-accredited version, which is a separate purchase. ACE sits in between: its default option is the most flexible and cheapest way in, with the accredited version available if and when you actually need it.

Why every comparison, including this one, deserves a skeptical read

NASM publishes its own NASM-vs-ACE-vs-ISSA comparison article. So does a competing certifier, NESTA. Nearly every "which cert is best" page online is published by a company selling one of the options being compared. That doesn't make the numbers wrong - the pricing above matched each certifier's own live page when checked - but it's worth remembering that "cheapest" and "best value" framing in this space almost always comes from someone with a stake in the answer.

FAQ

Which is cheaper: NASM, ACE, or ISSA?

ACE has the lowest floor - its exam-only option runs about $489-$499 if you already have your own study materials, though most people buy a package in the $745-$1,750 range. ISSA's official site currently prices its standard package $868-$1,068. NASM's packages typically run $999-$1,999. All three run frequent promotions, so the advertised price on any given day matters more than which brand is "cheapest" on paper.

What's the difference between NASM, ACE, and ISSA exams?

NASM's exam is proctored, closed-book, and in-person, with roughly an 85% first-attempt pass rate. ACE's default included exam is open-book and non-proctored (and not itself NCCA-accredited) - its NCCA-accredited version is a separate proctored add-on. ISSA's standard exam is open-book, untimed, and online, with its NCCA-accredited pathway offered separately through its NCCPT subsidiary.

Does it matter which certification I choose for getting hired?

For most commercial gyms, no - NASM, ACE, and ISSA are all widely recognized and will get you hired and insured. The bigger practical differences are exam format (do you want a proctored in-person test or a flexible open-book online one), price on the day you buy, and whether you want the NCCA-accredited version, which some employers or insurers specifically require.
