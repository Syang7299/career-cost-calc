DOT Physical Cost for CDL Drivers: Price & Requirements Guide

The DOT physical is one line item on our CDL calculator, but the price swings by 3x depending on where you get it - and a 2025 rule change means the paper card you're picturing isn't actually part of the process anymore.

CareerCost Guides · Updated September 2026

Our CDL cost calculator includes a DOT physical exam fee as one of four line items. This guide breaks down what actually drives that price, how often you'll pay it again, and a real regulatory change that changes what "getting your medical card" means in 2026.

How much a DOT physical costs

A DOT physical typically runs $50 to $150, and like most medical services, the price depends heavily on where you get it done. Most drivers end up paying $75-$110.

The drug test is a separate cost

The federally required DOT drug screen is not included in the physical exam price - it's billed separately, typically $30-$85. Many clinics bundle both into one visit for convenience, which is where you'll see combined pricing quoted anywhere from $80 to $235. If a quote you're given seems unusually low, confirm whether it includes the drug test or just the physical.

A few other fees to watch for: a specialist follow-up (only if the exam flags a specific issue) can run $100 or more; a vision or hearing referral, if your screening doesn't pass on the spot, typically runs $50-$200; and replacement medical certificate copies are usually free to $25.

How often you need to renew

The standard DOT medical certificate is valid for up to 2 years - but that's a maximum, not a guarantee. Drivers with certain manageable conditions (treated hypertension and insulin-treated diabetes are the two most common examples) are often certified for a shorter window, sometimes as little as 3-12 months, at the examining medical professional's discretion. There's no grace period once a certificate expires - you legally cannot operate a commercial vehicle on an expired medical certification, even with an exam appointment already booked.

The 2025 rule change: no more paper medical card

As of the FMCSA's Medical Examiner's Certification Integration rule, which reached full compliance on June 23, 2025, CDL and CLP holders no longer submit a paper Medical Examiner's Certificate to their state. Exam results are now sent electronically from the examining provider to the state licensing agency and posted directly to the driver's CDLIS Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) - which motor carriers and enforcement now check instead of a physical card. You still have to pass the same exam on the same schedule; what changed is the paperwork, not the medical requirement itself.

Ways to pay less (or nothing)

A few real options exist before you pay full price out of pocket:

  • Company-sponsored training - many carriers cover the DOT physical (and often the drug test) as part of a paid CDL training program, the same arrangement covered in our CDL cost calculator's company-sponsored path.
  • Your CDL school - some schools include the physical in tuition or have a discounted partner clinic.
  • WIOA or state workforce funding - where available, workforce development funding can cover CDL-related costs including the physical exam for eligible applicants.

FAQ

How much does a DOT physical cost for CDL drivers?

A DOT physical typically costs $50 to $150 depending on the provider - dedicated CDL/occupational health clinics run $50-$80, retail and pharmacy clinics run $80-$120, and urgent care centers run $80-$150. Most drivers pay $75-$110. The DOT drug test is billed separately, usually $30-$85, so a combined visit can run $80-$235 total.

How often do CDL drivers need to renew their DOT physical?

Most drivers renew every 2 years, which is the maximum certification period. Drivers with certain conditions - treated hypertension or insulin-treated diabetes are common examples - may be certified for a shorter period, often 3 to 12 months, at the examining provider's discretion. There is no grace period once a medical certificate expires.

Do CDL drivers still need to carry a paper medical card?

No, not as of the FMCSA's Medical Examiner's Certification Integration rule, which took full effect June 23, 2025. Medical examiner results are now submitted electronically and posted to the driver's CDLIS Motor Vehicle Record (MVR), which is the record motor carriers and enforcement now check - drivers still have to pass the exam, but carrying a separate paper card is no longer the compliance mechanism.
