Electrician & Lineman Apprenticeship Aptitude Test: ALBAT, SWLCAT & What to Expect

Before any of the wage-gap math on our electrician or lineman calculators applies to you, you have to clear a screening test most applicants don't know is coming. ALBAT, SWLCAT, and NEAT aren't different exams - they're the same test, run by different local programs.

CareerCost Guides · Updated September 2026

Our electrician and lineman calculators start from the assumption you're already in an apprenticeship. This guide covers the gate before that: the aptitude test nearly every IBEW/NECA apprenticeship program uses to screen applicants.

ALBAT, SWLCAT, and NEAT are the same test

These aren't three different exams - they're three different Joint Apprenticeship and Training Committees (JATCs) administering the same underlying Electrical Training Alliance aptitude test. ALBAT is the American Line Builders Area JATC, which runs outside-lineman apprenticeships across a band of Midwest and Mid-Atlantic states. SWLCAT is the Southwestern Line Constructors Apprenticeship and Training program. NEAT is the Northeastern JATC. Whichever local you apply through, the test format is the same - only the passing score and administrative details vary.

It's a proctored, computer-based test taken at a local training center - not something you can do from home. There's no calculator allowed, so the math section (linear equations, factoring and expanding, graph interpretation, PEMDAS-style problems) has to be worked by hand. The reading section tests how well you can pull specific facts from a dense passage under time pressure, not general vocabulary.

The passing score depends on your local

The test is scored on a scale of 1 to 9, and there's no single passing number - each local JATC sets its own bar based on demand for that year's apprenticeship class.

Minimum score to qualify for an interview

Scores are out of 9. A higher bar usually reflects more applicants than openings that year, not a harder version of the test.

How to actually prepare

The math section rewards speed as much as accuracy - 33 questions in 46 minutes is roughly 84 seconds per question, and that's tight if you're re-deriving formulas from scratch instead of recognizing the problem type immediately. Focus your review on:

  • Linear equations and PEMDAS-style arithmetic - the most common question type, and the fastest to get wrong under time pressure if you're rusty.
  • Factoring and expanding quadratic expressions - a specific skill worth drilling in isolation rather than relying on general algebra review.
  • Graph interpretation - reading values and relationships off a chart quickly, without needing to recalculate anything.
  • Timed reading practice - the reading section isn't about vocabulary, it's about locating specific facts in a passage fast enough to answer 36 questions in 51 minutes.

Since no calculator is allowed, the highest-value practice is doing arithmetic by hand under a timer, not just reviewing concepts. The Electrical Training Alliance's own sample test questions are the most representative free practice available, since they come from the organization that writes the real exam.

What happens if you fail

You can retake it, but not right away. Results typically take 2-4 weeks to come back, and most locals require a waiting period of roughly 3 to 6 months before you're eligible to retest - the exact rule varies by local JATC, so confirm the specific window with the program you applied to. Trying to work around the waiting period is generally treated as a rule violation and can risk disqualification, so it's worth using the wait productively: the Electrical Training Alliance's own site offers free sample test questions, and most of the score gap between a 3 and a 5 comes down to speed on the math section specifically, not raw ability.

FAQ

What are ALBAT and SWLCAT?

They're not different tests - they're the local Joint Apprenticeship and Training Committees (JATCs) that administer the same underlying Electrical Training Alliance aptitude exam. ALBAT is the American Line Builders Area JATC (outside lineman apprenticeships across the Midwest and Mid-Atlantic), and SWLCAT is the Southwestern Line Constructors Apprenticeship and Training program. Both use the same algebra/functions and reading comprehension test format.

What score do you need to pass the IBEW/electrical apprenticeship aptitude test?

The test is scored on a scale of 1 to 9, and the passing score to qualify for an interview varies by local. Most locals require a 4. Some locals with a high volume of applicants raise the bar to 5. Some programs, including NEAT (Northeastern JATC), only require a 3.

Can you retake the aptitude test if you fail?

Yes, but not immediately. Most locals require a waiting period of roughly 3 to 6 months before you can retake it, and results typically take 2-4 weeks to come back after you test. The exact rule varies by local JATC, so confirm the specific waiting period with the program you applied to.
