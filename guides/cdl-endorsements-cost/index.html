CDL Endorsements Cost: Hazmat, Tanker & Doubles/Triples

One of these three endorsements requires a federal background check and repeats every 5 years. The other two are close to a rounding error and never expire. Here's the real cost of each, and which ones are actually worth adding.

CareerCost Guides · Updated September 2026

Our CDL cost calculator covers the license itself. Endorsements are separate add-ons that open up specific routes and freight - here's what each one actually costs, and why one of the three behaves completely differently from the other two.

Hazmat: the expensive, recurring one

A Hazmat endorsement typically costs $115-$205 total, made up of three separate charges: ELDT theory training, a state DMV endorsement fee, and the federal piece - a TSA security threat assessment that costs $85.25 as of TSA's fee schedule effective January 1, 2025. If you already hold a valid TWIC card, you may qualify for a reduced $41 TSA rate.

Unlike the other two endorsements, Hazmat isn't a one-time cost. The background check is valid for 5 years, and renewing means resubmitting fingerprints and paying the fee again - so budget for it as a recurring cost, not a single upfront one. TSA also recommends applying at least 60 days before you need it, since processing can take over 45 days.

Typical cost by endorsement

Tanker and doubles/triples are one-time; Hazmat's cost recurs every 5 years with a new background check.

Both tanker and doubles/triples just require passing a knowledge test at your state DMV - no federal background check, no training course requirement beyond studying for the test, and no renewal fee once you pass. That's the entire reason the cost gap with Hazmat is so large: Hazmat's price is mostly a federal security fee, not a training or testing cost. If you're weighing whether a route is worth it, that distinction matters more than the sticker price alone - a $25 tanker endorsement is a low-risk add-on almost regardless of whether you end up using it, while a $205 Hazmat endorsement is a real commitment with a real wait.

Endorsement letter codes, and common combinations

Endorsements show up on your CDL as single letters: H for Hazmat, N for tanker, T for doubles/triples, plus P (passenger) and S (school bus) for driver categories not covered in this guide. Some freight - fuel hauling in particular - requires the combined X endorsement (Hazmat + tanker together), which just means passing both individual knowledge tests plus the Hazmat background check; there's no separate combo fee beyond the two individual costs added together.

The ELDT (Entry-Level Driver Training) theory portion required for Hazmat is typically bundled into the same online or in-person training block many drivers already do for their base CDL, so it often adds modest incremental cost rather than a full separate course - confirm with your specific school whether Hazmat theory is included or billed separately before assuming the low end of the range applies to you.

Is it worth adding an endorsement speculatively?

For tanker and doubles/triples, yes - the cost is low enough and the renewal is nonexistent, so there's little downside to adding either if there's any chance you'll want that freight later. Hazmat is a different calculation: it's a real, recurring cost with a slow federal processing time, so it makes more sense to add once you have a specific job or route in mind that requires it, rather than getting it "just in case."

Budget note: none of these are included in our CDL calculator's default total, since not every driver needs an endorsement. Add $10-$205 depending on which ones your target job requires.

FAQ

How much does a Hazmat endorsement cost?

Most drivers pay $115-$205 total: the TSA's own security threat assessment fee is $85.25 (per TSA's official fee schedule, effective January 1, 2025), plus ELDT theory training and a state DMV endorsement fee that varies by state. Drivers who already hold a valid TWIC card may qualify for a reduced $41 TSA rate.

Do tanker and doubles/triples endorsements expire?

No. Unlike Hazmat, which requires a new TSA background check and fee every 5 years, the tanker (N) and doubles/triples (T) endorsements are typically one-time - pass the knowledge test once and it stays on your CDL with no separate renewal fee.

Is it worth getting a Hazmat endorsement?

It depends on the routes available to you. Hazmat-endorsed drivers are often eligible for specific freight that pays a premium, but the $115-$205 cost recurs every 5 years and the TSA background check can take over 45 days to process, so it's worth pursuing only if you have a specific job or route in mind that requires it - not as a speculative add-on.
