Using the GI Bill for CDL, Electrician & Trade Training

The GI Bill covers all six careers on this site, but not the same way. School programs and apprenticeships are paid completely differently - and the apprenticeship version has a declining payment schedule most guides gloss over.

CareerCost Guides · Updated September 2026

Whether you're looking at our CDL, cosmetology, electrician, or lineman calculator, GI Bill benefits can offset a real chunk of the total cost - the mechanics just depend on which kind of program you're in. The school has to be approved by its state's VA approving agency to accept GI Bill students at all, so confirm that status before enrolling, not after.

Two different payment structures

The GI Bill treats standalone vocational schools and apprenticeships as fundamentally different tracks:

  • Vocational school (CDL, cosmetology): the VA pays your tuition and fees directly to the school, plus a monthly housing allowance (MHA) prorated by your weekly clock hours, plus up to $1,000/year for books and supplies. This works like a standard school benefit.
  • Apprenticeship / on-the-job training (electrician, lineman): there's no tuition to pay, since you're earning wages from an employer or union instead. The VA pays a monthly housing allowance on top of your apprentice wages - but that stipend declines the longer you're in the program.

How the vocational-school track works in practice

For a standalone program like CDL or cosmetology school, the VA sets your MHA rate based on how many clock hours per week you're scheduled to attend - full-time, three-quarter time, half-time, or less than half-time - not a flat rate for every student. A full-time CDL program condensed into 4 weeks and a part-time cosmetology program spread over a year will draw very different total MHA payments even if the underlying hourly rate is the same, since the schedule changes how many months you're actually enrolled and drawing the benefit. Tuition and fees are paid to the school directly regardless of your hours tier, so that part of your CDL or cosmetology calculator total can effectively drop to $0 if the program is fully covered.

The 36-month entitlement limit is a real constraint

Most veterans have 36 months of GI Bill entitlement total, shared across every program they use it for - not per program. A 3-4 year electrician or lineman apprenticeship can run longer than your remaining entitlement, especially if you've already used some benefit for prior schooling. Check your actual remaining months (via your VA eBenefits account) before assuming the stipend covers your entire apprenticeship.

Who's eligible: veterans and current reservists who qualify for the GI Bill, plus dependents using transferred Post-9/11 benefits, the Fry Scholarship, or Dependents' Educational Assistance. Active-duty service members and spouses of active-duty members using transferred benefits are not eligible for this specific benefit.

FAQ

Does the GI Bill cover CDL training, electrician apprenticeships, and cosmetology school?

Yes, all three are eligible, but the payment structure depends on the program type. Standalone vocational programs (CDL school, cosmetology school) get tuition and fees paid directly, plus a prorated monthly housing allowance and a book stipend. Apprenticeships and on-the-job training (electrician, lineman) work differently - there's no tuition to pay since you're earning wages, so the VA instead pays a declining monthly housing allowance on top of your apprentice wages.

How does the GI Bill housing allowance work for an apprenticeship?

It starts at 100% of the applicable housing allowance for your first 6 months, then steps down to 80% for months 7-12, 60% for months 13-18, 40% for months 19-24, and 20% for the remainder of training. This is on top of the wages your employer or union already pays you as an apprentice.

How long can you use the GI Bill for trade training?

Most veterans have 36 months of GI Bill entitlement total, shared across all programs you use it for. A 3-4 year electrician or lineman apprenticeship can exceed that limit, so check your remaining entitlement before assuming the benefit covers your entire program.
