Real Estate Broker License: Cost & Experience Requirements by State

Most states ask for 2 years of agent experience before you can upgrade to broker. Texas asks for 4 - and just doubled its points requirement on top of that for 2026, a change that matters right now if you're mid-way through building your own experience toward it.

Years of agent experience required before you can apply for broker

California, New York, and Florida cluster around 2 years. Texas requires double that, plus a points-based experience system.

Texas just doubled its points requirement for 2026

Texas measures broker experience through a points system, not years alone - you need real transactions, not just time elapsed. Per TREC's own announcement, effective January 1, 2026, the minimum required experience points doubled from 360 to 720.

Texas broker experience points required

TREC also capped bachelor's-degree point substitution at 300 (down from 630), shifting weight toward real transaction experience over academic credit.

A closed residential sale earns 30 points under the current scale, so 720 points works out to roughly 24 qualifying transactions spread across your minimum 4 years of active experience - real deal volume, not just time served. An agent averaging fewer than 6 closed deals a year could hit the 4-year time requirement well before hitting the point requirement - a real gap between "years licensed" and "years actually selling," and part of why TREC's own framing for the 2026 change emphasizes real transaction experience over time served or academic substitution alone.

What a broker license actually lets you do

An agent legally has to work under a licensed broker - every transaction you close is technically supervised. A broker license removes that requirement: you can operate independently, open your own brokerage, hold other agents' license under your supervision, and handle client trust funds directly, none of which a standard salesperson license permits. That's the real reason most agents eventually pursue it, separate from any income bump - it's the difference between working under someone else's business and running your own.

Not every broker opens a brokerage immediately. Many get the license specifically to operate independently without changing much else about their day-to-day work, while others use it as the required step before building a team of agents under them. Either way, the license itself doesn't require you to change how you work - it just removes the legal requirement to work under someone else.

What the upgrade actually costs

Total cost to upgrade typically runs $1,450-$1,850: roughly $649 in state exam, application, and background check fees, plus $800-$1,200 for the required broker-level coursework. That coursework is genuinely more in-depth than what you took for your original license - covering brokerage management, trust account handling, and real estate law - not a repeat of your agent-level material.

FAQ

How many years of experience do you need to become a real estate broker?

It depends on the state. California, New York, and Florida all require 2 years of active salesperson experience within the preceding 5 years. Texas requires 4 years - double the others - measured through a points system rather than years alone.

How much does it cost to upgrade from agent to broker?

Total cost typically runs $1,450-$1,850: roughly $649 in state exam, application, and background check fees, plus $800-$1,200 for the required broker pre-license coursework, which is more in-depth than agent-level courses and covers brokerage management, trust accounts, and real estate law.

What changed for Texas real estate brokers in 2026?

Effective January 1, 2026, the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC) doubled the minimum experience points required for a broker license from 360 to 720, and capped how many points a bachelor's degree can substitute for at 300 (down from 630) - shifting the requirement toward real transaction experience and away from academic credit.
