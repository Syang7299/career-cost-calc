California Real Estate License Cost: DRE Fees & Total Breakdown

What actually varies: your pre-licensing course

California requires 135 hours of DRE-approved pre-licensing education, typically split across three courses - Real Estate Principles, Real Estate Practice, and one elective. Course pricing is where your total cost actually swings: anywhere from about $100 to $600 depending on the school, format, and included materials.

Cost breakdown

Fingerprint, exam, and license fees are fixed by DRE. Only the course cost is a real range - shop around, since the spread is wide for the same 135-hour requirement.

Add DRE's fixed $499 to a $100-$600 course and your real total lands at roughly $599 to $1,099 - close to the $600-$1,100 figure most sources quote, but now you know exactly which $499 of it is fixed and which part you actually control. A cheaper course isn't automatically a worse choice - the exam itself is the same regardless of which approved provider you learned from, so it often comes down to format (self-paced online vs. live classes) and how much you value included exam-prep materials, not the underlying content quality.

Renewal: a recurring cost every 4 years

Your license isn't a one-time purchase. California requires 45 hours of continuing education every 4 years to renew - your first renewal requires 45 hours split into 35 required class hours and 10 elective hours; subsequent renewals require a survey course covering ethics, agency, fair housing, and trust fund handling (9 hours), consumer protection courses (18 hours minimum), and additional consumer-service coursework to round out the 45. The renewal fee itself is $350 on time, $525 if you let it lapse and file late - the same $350 as your original license fee, so budget for it as a recurring cost, not something you only pay once.

Be skeptical of California-specific income numbers

Search "California real estate agent first year income" and you'll find numbers from $20,000 all the way to $84,000, often on the same course-provider's own site. That's not a coincidence - the businesses selling pre-licensing courses have a direct financial incentive to make the career look as lucrative as possible. Nationally, NAR data puts the median income for agents with under 2 years of experience around $8,100, with a realistic first-year range closer to $10,000-$35,000 for someone building a client base from scratch.

California's higher home prices can mean a bigger commission per deal, but that doesn't automatically mean a higher first-year income if deal volume is lower while you're new. FAQ

How much does a California real estate license cost?

California's DRE charges exactly $499 in direct state fees: a $100 exam fee, a $350 original license fee, and a $49 fingerprint processing fee. Add a required 135-hour pre-licensing course, which typically runs $100-$600 depending on the provider, for a real total of roughly $599 to $1,099.

How many hours of pre-licensing education does California require?

California requires 135 hours of DRE-approved pre-licensing coursework, typically split across three courses: Real Estate Principles, Real Estate Practice, and one elective course, before you're eligible to sit for the salesperson exam.

How much do new real estate agents make in their first year in California?

Be skeptical of any single number here - course providers have a financial incentive to quote optimistic figures, and estimates across sources range enormously, from under $25,000 for part-time agents to $50,000+ for full-time agents with a strong network. Nationally, NAR data puts the median income for agents under 2 years around $8,100.
