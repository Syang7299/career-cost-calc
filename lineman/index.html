How this calculator works

Becoming a lineman has two optional cost layers. First, a pre-apprenticeship school is a one-time upfront cost some candidates pay to become a more competitive applicant - it's genuinely optional, so set it to $0 if you're hired directly by a utility or union local. Second, once you're in a paid apprenticeship, the real cost is the wage gap between what you earn during training and what you'd make elsewhere (this calculator assumes a straight-line ramp from your starting % to 100%, a simplification of the real step-raise schedule but close enough for planning) - plus union dues along the way.

Once you're a full journeyman, the payback formula is the same one used everywhere on this site: Payback Period = Total Cost ÷ Annual Wage Gain (journeyman pay vs. your current wage, both annualized at 2,000 hours/year).

What's in the numbers

Pre-apprenticeship school (optional)

Private lineman schools run $399-$18,000 (median ~$8,400) for a 5-15 week program - skip this entirely if you're hired directly into an apprenticeship.

Wage ramp

Apprentices typically start at 50-60% of journeyman wage and get step raises as they accumulate hours, reaching 100% at completion - typically 3-4 years.

Union dues / apprenticeship fees

IBEW local dues during an apprenticeship commonly run $500-$700/year - a real cost, but far less than trade-school tuition since you're earning while you learn.

Journeyman wage gain

The permanent raise you get once licensed - median journeyman lineman pay is $95,320/year (BLS) - this is what actually pays back your training cost, year after year.

FAQ

How much does it cost to become a lineman?

It depends heavily on your path. If you attend a private pre-apprenticeship lineman school first, tuition runs $399 to about $18,000 (median around $8,400) for a 5-15 week program, plus $1,000+ for tools and boots. If you're hired directly into a utility or union apprenticeship without lineman school, that upfront cost is $0 - your main cost becomes union dues (typically $500-$700/year) and any wage gap during the 3-4 year apprenticeship.

Do you have to pay for lineman school before starting an apprenticeship?

No. Lineman school (also called a pre-apprenticeship program) is optional - it makes you a more competitive candidate for a limited number of apprenticeship openings, but many linemen are hired directly by a utility or IBEW local and trained entirely on the job, paying nothing upfront for school.

How long does a lineman apprenticeship take and how much do apprentices get paid?

Most apprenticeships run 3 to 4 years. Apprentices typically start around 50-60% of journeyman wage and receive step raises as they accumulate hours, reaching full journeyman pay (median $95,320/year per the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics) at completion.
