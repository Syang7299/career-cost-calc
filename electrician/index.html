How this calculator works

An apprenticeship's real cost is mostly the wage gap, not tuition - you earn a rising percentage of journeyman wage during training (this calculator assumes a straight-line ramp from your starting % to 100%, which is a simplification of the real step-raise schedule but close enough for planning). If that average apprentice wage is still below what you'd earn elsewhere, that shortfall - plus program fees, tools, and the exam fee - is your total cost.

Once you're a full journeyman, the payback formula is the same one used everywhere on this site: Payback Period = Total Cost ÷ Annual Wage Gain (journeyman pay vs. your current wage, both annualized at 2,000 hours/year).

What's in the numbers

Wage ramp

Apprentices start at 40-50% of journeyman wage and get raises roughly every 1,000 hours, reaching 100% at completion - typically around 4 years.

Program fees

Annual enrollment for a registered apprenticeship program, typically $250-$1,420/year - far less than trade-school tuition since you're earning while you learn.

Tools & books

Hand tools, a code book, and course materials - a real cost, but usually a fraction of what tuition-based trade school runs.

Journeyman wage gain

The permanent raise you get once licensed - this is what actually pays back your training cost, year after year.

FAQ

How much does it cost to become a licensed electrician?

Most electricians train through a paid apprenticeship rather than paying upfront tuition. Program enrollment fees typically run $250 to $1,420 per year over a 4-year apprenticeship, plus $500 to $10,000 for tools and books, plus a licensing exam fee of roughly $30 to $75. The bigger factor is usually the wage gap - apprentices earn 40-50% of journeyman wage in year one, rising to 100% at completion.

How much do electrician apprentices get paid compared to journeymen?

Apprentices typically start at 40-50% of the local journeyman wage and get raises roughly every 1,000 on-the-job hours (about every 6 months), reaching 80-90% by years 4-5 and 100% (full journeyman pay) at completion. A 4-year apprenticeship averages out to roughly 70-75% of journeyman wage over the whole program.

How long does an electrician apprenticeship take?

Most apprenticeship programs take 4 years, combining around 2,000 hours per year of on-the-job training with about 144 hours per year of classroom instruction.
