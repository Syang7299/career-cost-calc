How this calculator works

Your total cost to get a CDL is school tuition plus the state permit, skills test, and license fees, plus your DOT physical - four numbers that vary by school and state, so this calculator uses your real quotes instead of a single national average.

Payback period uses the same formula as every calculator on this site: Total Cost ÷ Annual Income Gain. If CDL school costs $6,400 total and you'll earn $48,000 more per year than you do now, that pays back in weeks, not years - which is exactly why trucking is often pitched as a fast-payback career change.

What's in the total cost

School tuition

The training program itself. Community colleges run $3,000-$7,000; private schools run $4,000-$8,000 for a 3-7 week course.

Permit & license fees

State charges for your Commercial Learner's Permit and CDL issuance - typically $50-$250 combined, varying widely by state.

Skills test fee

The road test that actually earns you the CDL, separate from school tuition - typically $100-$250.

DOT physical exam

A required medical exam, typically $75-$150 - often free or discounted through your school, a carrier, or WIOA funding.

FAQ

How much does it cost to get a CDL?

Self-paid CDL school runs $3,000 to $10,000 - community colleges typically $3,000-$7,000, private schools $4,000-$8,000 for a 3-7 week course. On top of tuition, budget $75-$300 in state permit, license, and skills test fees, plus a $75-$150 DOT physical exam.

Can you get a CDL for free through company-sponsored training?

Yes. Many carriers pay you to train (often several hundred dollars a week) instead of charging tuition, in exchange for a signed commitment to drive for them - typically 12 to 24 months. Leaving early usually means repaying a prorated share of the training cost, often $3,000-$7,000, so it isn't actually free if you don't finish the contract.

How much do first-year CDL drivers make?

Most new CDL-A drivers start between $35,000 and $58,000 in their first year, commonly paid per mile (roughly $0.40-$0.55/mile for rookies), with pay typically increasing at the 6-month and 1-year marks.
